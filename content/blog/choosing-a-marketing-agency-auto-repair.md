Hiring a marketing agency is a little like hiring a mechanic you've never met to work on your own car. You're trusting someone with real money and you can't fully verify the work until after it's done, and by then the "new serpentine belt" has already been billed either way. Get it wrong and you don't just lose the budget, you lose months of slow bays you can't get back.

Here's what actually separates a good agency from a bad one, in terms you don't need a marketing degree to evaluate.

## Industry experience actually matters here

A generic marketing agency can run ads for a dentist on Monday and a landscaper on Tuesday. That's not automatically bad, but it means every campaign starts from zero. An agency that only works with auto repair shops already knows which services convert best, what a reasonable cost per booked appointment looks like, and how to write an ad that doesn't sound like it was written for a restaurant with "repair shop" swapped in, find-and-replace is not a marketing strategy.

**Ask them:** "What other auto repair shops have you worked with, and what results did they see?" A real answer sounds like numbers and specifics. A vague answer sounds like "oh, we've done a lot of local businesses."

## Month-to-month, or locked in?

This is one of the biggest red flags in the whole industry. A lot of agencies push a 6 or 12-month contract before you've seen a single result. That arrangement protects the agency's revenue, not your business, which is a strange thing to pay extra for.

A confident agency doesn't need to lock you in, because they expect to keep earning your business every month on results alone. If an agency insists on a long contract before proving anything, ask yourself why they need that insurance policy.

**Ask them:** "What happens if I want to leave after the first month?" The answer tells you a lot about how confident they actually are.

## Can they show you real numbers, or just activity?

Almost any agency can hand you a report full of impressions, clicks, and reach. Those numbers are easy to make look good and don't actually tell you if you made money, a dashboard full of green arrows has never once paid a technician's wage. What you want is a report that connects straight back to your business: how many appointments came from the campaign, and what you spent to get them.

**Ask them:** "Can I see a sample report?" If it's full of vanity metrics and light on appointments and cost per lead, that's worth noticing before you sign anything, not after.

## Who's actually doing the work?

Some agencies sell you on a senior strategist in the pitch meeting, then hand your account to whoever's newest once you've signed. Ask directly who will be managing your campaigns day to day, and how often you'll actually hear from them. A shop owner who only hears from their agency when it's time to renew is a shop owner who has no idea what's actually happening with their money.

**Ask them:** "Who will I actually be talking to, and how often?"

## Red flags worth walking away from

- **Guarantees that sound too specific.** Nobody can promise an exact number of appointments before they've even started, since too much depends on your shop, your area, and your current reviews.
- **Pressure to sign today.** A good fit doesn't expire by tomorrow, your transmission didn't need replacing "right this second, sir" either, and you were right to be suspicious then too.
- **No clear answer on what counts as success.** If they can't tell you how they'll measure whether it's working, they haven't thought it through either.
- **Radio silence after signing.** The sales call was warm and responsive, then suddenly updates get hard to get. That pattern tends to continue, not improve.

## A short list of questions to bring to any agency

1. Do you work with other auto repair shops, and can I see real results?
2. Is there a long-term contract, or can I leave month to month?
3. What exactly will I see in my reporting?
4. Who's managing my account, and how often will I hear from them?
5. How do you decide what counts as a win?

Any agency worth hiring will answer these plainly, without getting defensive. If a question makes someone squirm, that's useful information too.
