Here's something that surprises a lot of shop owners the first time they see it: Google Ads doesn't just show your ad for the exact phrase you picked. It shows it for anything Google thinks is related, which sometimes includes searches that were never going to turn into a customer no matter how good your ad is. **Negative keywords** are how you tell Google which searches to leave your ad out of.

It's one of the simplest ways to stop paying for clicks that were never going to book, and most shops never touch it.

## What a negative keyword actually does

When you target a keyword like "brake repair," Google doesn't just match that exact phrase. It also tries to match similar searches it judges to be related, which can include things you never intended to pay for. A negative keyword is an instruction that says "never show my ad for this search," even if Google thinks it's a close match.

## Searches that waste money without you noticing

A handful of search types show up again and again in auto repair accounts that haven't been cleaned up:

- **"How to" and "DIY" searches.** Someone searching "how to replace brake pads yourself" is looking for a YouTube video and a sense of regret two hours later, not a shop.
- **Job searches.** "Brake repair jobs" or "mechanic jobs near me" has nothing to do with a customer, that's someone looking for employment, not a transmission rebuild.
- **"Free" searches.** Unless you actually offer a free inspection, "free brake inspection" clicks are rarely going to convert, nothing in life is as expensive as the word "free" searched by the wrong person.
- **Parts and manuals.** "Brake pad replacement manual" or "used brake parts" are DIY and resale searches, not service searches.
- **Other brands.** If you don't service a specific manufacturer's dealership-only work, excluding that brand name can filter out searches you were never going to win anyway.

None of these clicks are expensive to notice individually. Add them up over a few months, across several ad groups, and they quietly account for a real chunk of wasted spend.

## How to actually find them

Inside Google Ads, there's a report called **Search Terms** that shows the exact phrases people typed before your ad showed up and they clicked. It's the single most useful, most underused report in the whole platform. Reviewing it monthly and adding the obvious non-customer searches as negative keywords is the entire process, there's no complicated strategy beyond actually looking at the list.

## A simple way to think about it

If a search term would never reasonably end in a booked appointment, no matter how perfect your ad and landing page are, it belongs on the negative list. If there's a real chance it converts, even a modest one, leave it alone.

## Common mistakes

- **Never checking the search terms report at all.** This is the single biggest miss, the data showing exactly where money is leaking sits there unused, like a dashboard warning light nobody's bothered to look up.
- **Being too aggressive.** Excluding too broadly can accidentally block real customers along with the noise. When in doubt, exclude the obviously irrelevant phrase, not the general topic.
- **Setting it once and forgetting it.** New irrelevant searches show up over time as Google's matching evolves. A quick monthly check catches most of it.
- **Confusing this with targeting.** Negative keywords don't improve what you're trying to attract, they just remove what you were never trying to attract in the first place.

## A quick example

If "free brake inspection" is getting clicked ten times a month at $4 a click, and none of those ten ever book, that's $40 spent on exactly nothing, every single month, until someone adds it as a negative keyword. Small individually, real over a year, and completely avoidable once it's on your radar, the marketing equivalent of a slow leak nobody noticed until the tire was flat.

## Where to start this week

1. **Open the Search Terms report** in your Google Ads account.
2. **Scan for anything obviously irrelevant**: DIY, jobs, free, manuals, parts.
3. **Add the clear non-customer searches** as negative keywords.
4. **Set a recurring monthly reminder** to check again, since new irrelevant searches show up over time.
